## Trail Mechanics Design (Evidence, Time, and Grounded Pursuit)

Trail mechanics are the missing link between:

- pure hidden movement (too opaque)
- constant exact detection (too easy)

A good chase game needs **partial persistence**:

The target leaves traces, but they decay.
The player is always working with _recent history_, not certainty.

This section defines how the player can track a moving target without directly seeing it.

---

# 1. Core Concept: The Target Leaves Evidence

Whenever the target moves through a system, it may leave behind:

- engine residue
- encrypted comm bursts
- disturbed traffic patterns
- sensor echoes

This becomes a **trail**, which the player can discover later.

Trail = “The target was here recently.”

Not “The target is here now.”

---

# 2. Trail States (Simple and Readable)

Each star system can be in one of these evidence states:

### No Trace

Nothing detectable.

### Fresh Trail

Target passed through very recently.

### Fading Trail

Target was here, but several turns ago.

### Cold Trail

Too old to be useful, soon disappears.

This is easy to communicate visually on a small screen.

---

# 3. Trail Lifetime

A typical timeline:

- Fresh: lasts 1 turn
- Fading: lasts 2 turns
- Cold: lasts 1 turn
- Then gone

Total persistence: **4 turns**

This creates urgency:

- act quickly or lose the lead

Difficulty lever:

- Easy mode: 5–6 turns persistence
- Hard mode: 2–3 turns persistence

---

# 4. How Trails Are Discovered

Trails are not automatically visible everywhere.
They require specific actions or proximity.

---

## A. Scout Investigation (Primary Trail Tool)

When a Scout investigates a system, it reports:

- Fresh trail → “Target passed through this turn or last.”
- Fading trail → “Target was here recently.”
- None → “No activity detected.”

This makes scouts the main trackers.

Cost: time + commitment.

---

## B. Passive Trail Detection (Close Range)

If the target passed through a system adjacent to a Scout, the Scout may detect a weak echo automatically:

- “Signal residue detected nearby.”

This gives the player a hint without full confirmation.

---

## C. Beacon Trigger + Trail Combination

Beacons detect presence when target enters range.
But if target leaves before interception, the beacon area becomes a trail zone:

- “Target moved through monitored corridor.”

Beacons don’t catch, they **anchor the timeline**.

---

## D. Survey Scan Reveals Trail Density

Wide sweeps do not locate the target directly, but can reveal:

- “Fresh disturbances detected in Sector C.”

This points scouts toward the right cluster.

---

# 5. Trail Information Is Directional (Advanced Depth)

To make pursuit richer, trails can sometimes indicate direction.

Example outputs:

- “Exhaust plume indicates departure toward Hub Vega.”
- “Signal drift suggests movement rimward.”

Direction clues should be probabilistic, not perfect.

This enables prediction instead of simple follow-the-dot.

---

# 6. Trail Strength Depends on Target Type

Different targets leave different signatures:

### Courier

- Strong, obvious trail (fast engines)

### Smuggler

- Medium trail, sometimes masked

### Ghost Probe

- Weak trail, decays faster

### Decoy Master

- Leaves false trails occasionally

This makes target identity matter.

---

# 7. Target Counterplay: Trail Suppression

Targets should have limited stealth abilities, not permanent invisibility.

Examples:

## A. Silent Running (Cooldown Ability)

Target can reduce trail strength for 2 turns.

Player sees:

- “Sensors report unusual quiet.”

Creates uncertainty spikes.

## B. Nebula Scrub Zones

If target enters nebula systems:

- trails decay 1 turn faster
- scans less effective

Nebula becomes a tactical hiding environment.

---

# 8. The Trail as a Time Window

The player is not locating the target, but locating its _recent past_.

The chase becomes:

- Find fresh trail
- Predict next junction
- Deploy interceptor ahead
- Trap at chokepoint

The player should rarely catch by following directly.
They win by cutting off.

---

# 9. Trail-Based Player Decisions

Trail mechanics create meaningful choices:

### Do I investigate the fresh trail system…

or sprint interceptor to the next chokepoint?

### Do I spend energy on a wide scan…

or commit scouts deeper?

### Do I deploy a beacon now…

or save kits for the endgame?

The trail is the pressure forcing action.

---

# 10. False Trails and Noise (Controlled)

To avoid perfect certainty:

- fading trails may sometimes be ambiguous
- decoy targets may drop one fake trail per mission

Important: false trails must be rare and signaled, or it feels unfair.

Example warning:

- “Possible signal spoofing detected.”

---

# 11. Typical Chase Sequence With Trails

Turn 6:

- Scout investigates Hub A → Fresh trail found

Turn 7:

- Player predicts target will cross Chokepoint B
- Beacon deployed at B

Turn 8:

- Beacon triggers → target confirmed passed corridor
- Trail now fading behind

Turn 9:

- Interceptor races ahead to Hub C
- Scout confirms no trail elsewhere

Turn 10:

- Target enters Hub C
- Interceptor intercepts → win

This feels like hunting, not guessing.

---

# 12. Visual Communication (320×240 Friendly)

Trails must be readable without clutter:

- Fresh trail: bright ring pulse
- Fading trail: dim ring
- Cold trail: tiny marker

Only show trails when player has discovered them.
Keep map clean.

---

# 13. Difficulty Tuning Knobs

Trail mechanics provide many balance controls:

- Trail lifetime (2–6 turns)
- Chance of directional clue
- Nebula decay modifier
- Target stealth cooldown frequency
- Scout investigation speed

These are better than simply making the target faster.

---

# 14. Why Trails Complete the Design

Without trails:

- chase becomes blind and frustrating

With permanent detection:

- chase becomes trivial

With decaying trails:

- player has enough information to reason
- but must act under uncertainty and time pressure

The game becomes:

**Evidence → Prediction → Cutoff → Interception**

That is the intended core loop.

---

The next design layer is: **Mission Templates** (different win/loss structures beyond “catch before escape”), which will greatly increase variety with minimal additional systems.
